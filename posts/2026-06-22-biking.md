# Somerville Community Path

I use this to commute to work from Magoun Square to Sullivan Square. It's one of the most famous bike paths around here, and for good reason! It's always super popular in the evenings and weekends -- although it gets crowded, it's a really great feeling to see so much of my local community every evening on my way home. The path is popular with everyone from regular bike commuters to small children learning how to bike for the first time, from people on intense long runs to those just out for a walk with their friends. It's really lovely to see so many people enjoying a community space in so many different ways.

It's hard for me to describe the presence that the community path has within the city of Somerville. It runs the entire length of the city, past dense squares like Davis and through quiet neighborhoods of triple-deckers. It's legitimately how I navigate -- even in the rare occasion I'm driving a car, I still use the roadway crossings as waypoints to understand where in the city I am. The bike path [even has its own mayor](https://www.reddit.com/r/Catswithjobs/comments/1mm393y/cat_mayor_of_somerville_ma_bike_path_election_2025/)!

<Review title="Car Interactions" stars={4}>

Overall this is managed really well! Crossings generally are elevated or have speed tables for crossing vehicles. The path route is entirely separated from traffic other than the at-grade crossings.

</Review>

<Review title="Lane Design" stars={5}>

The asphalt/concrete is in great condition in most places. The path can be quite crowded during the evening rush hour, and the width is constrained east of Magoun Square and near Davis Square. The East Somerville and Davis T station entrances open directly onto the path, causing significant crowding when trains arrive and unload passengers.

</Review>

<Review title="Tricky Sections" stars={3}>

There's no good bike route through Davis Square, where the train station and a plaza interrupt the path. The path has two major hills: one at the transition from street-level down to "train-level" near the McGrath overpass, and one very high bridge over the green line tracks near Lechmere.

</Review>

<Review title="Connections/Usefulness" stars={5}>

The path connects to the Cambridge Linear Park and to North Point Park in Boston, giving easy access to the city via the new North Washington St bridge. It also passes through Davis Square, Gilman Square, and Brickbottom, and goes near Porter Square and Union Square.

</Review>

<Review title="Favorite part" stars={5}>

The section between Walnut St and the Cambridge Crossing neighborhood is 1.3 miles of entirely grade-separated path, meaning no intersections with cars slowing you down!

</Review>

# Hampshire St. / Beacon St. (Cambridge/Somerville)

This route links my apartment to Inman, Kendall, and Central Square. I was quite happy when I first discovered it -- it's a really nice neighborhood corridor! It's usually my route when I go to Swapfest at MIT every month. While riding on separated paths is much nicer, these roads definitely showcase how well-designed bicycle infrastructure can create a lovely bicycle route along a roadway shared with cars.

<Review title="Car Interactions" stars={4}>

It's really good for a non-separated path! The bike lane is protected for most of the length with ample daylighting at intersections.

</Review>

<Review title="Lane Design" stars={4}>

The lane is well-designed, well-marked and mostly free of potholes. It's generally not too crowded, but passing slower cyclists is often not practical due to the lane width and it being a protected lane.

</Review>

<Review title="Tricky Sections" stars={4}>

The terminus at Somerville Ave is an awkward intersection that thankfully will get some much-needed improvements with the [Elm-Beacon Connector Project](https://voice.somervillema.gov/elm-beacon-connector). Some segments are unprotected/unbuffered, but the entire route has a separated lane.

</Review>

<Review title="Connections/Usefulness" stars={4}>

The path goes from Kendall Square to Porter Square via Inman Square.

</Review>

<Review title="Favorite part" stars={5}>

The bicycle infrastructure at Inman Square is very well designed and easy to bike through, with most conflicts with cars entirely eliminated through phases and signals. I love the cute little 4" traffic lights in the bike lane!

</Review>

# Broadway (Somerville)

This is another route I take to work sometimes! It's about a minute or two faster than taking the community path. It adds a pretty substantial hill, which is a fun challenge to get up and even more fun to zoom down. On a weekday morning, I'll often fly past a long line of cars on my way in! A wide road like Broadway definitely doesn't have the same cozy vibes as narrow streets passing through quiet neighborhoods, but it's a surprisingly comfortable experience to bike on nonetheless.

<Review title="Car Interactions" stars={4}>

It's a shared bus/bike lane for much of the length, which usually great (lots of space!) but can be tricky when the 89 or 101 bus is present.

</Review>

<Review title="Lane Design" stars={3}>

The lane is mostly okay, but the pavement is a bit rough in the Magoun Square area, and the design of that intersection isn't great. (There is a [reconstruction project](https://voice.somervillema.gov/broadway-magoun-reconstruction) planned however!) I've never seen it crowded, but it's also easy to pass in the wide bus lane.

</Review>

<Review title="Tricky Sections" stars={4}>

Going towards Sullivan Square is easy, but coming from it is much harder due to the one-way streets crossing the Orange Line. The route also goes directly over the summit of Winter Hill.

</Review>

<Review title="Connections/Usefulness" stars={4}>

This route Sullivan Square, the Winter Hill neighborhood, Magoun Square, and Ball Square.

</Review>

<Review title="Favorite part" stars={5}>

Having a full bus lane of width makes coasting down Winter Hill tons of fun!

</Review>

# Beacham/Williams St. (Chelsea)

I use this mostly to get to the airport, either when I'm traveling with just a backpack or when I'm meeting someone there. On that note: pople say I'm crazy for biking to the airport, but it's shockingly practical! E-bikes are now common at the BlueBikes bikeshare docks which makes the journey a bit easier, and there are plenty of docks in East Boston within walking distance of the airport.

A fun fact about the free Massport shuttles at Logan is that they run 24 hours a day and always stop at the MBTA station, even when the trains aren't running. That MBTA station is a short walk away from several BlueBikes docking stations!

<Review title="Car Interactions" stars={3}>

Part of it is a two-way separated path, but with large gaps for truck bays. The other half is relatively narrow lanes on a two-way road. Trucks are present 24/7, which can be tricky to navigate late at night.

</Review>

<Review title="Lane Design" stars={4}>

The path part is pretty good, it's been very empty every time I've been there. There are some utility poles that need to be dodged, I think there was initially meant to be a project to relocate them but I don't know if that ever happened. The road parts aren't too bad, since the road is pretty low-traffic.

</Review>

<Review title="Tricky Sections" stars={4}>

There's not great bike infrastructure on either side of the path unfortunately. One end is Alford St near Encore, which is a wide road with narrow bike lanes. The other end is the residential side of Chelsea, which has little to offer cyclists other than sharrows.

</Review>

<Review title="Connections/Usefulness" stars={4}>

It's great for getting to the airport and one of the few routes through that area. That said, it mostly goes through an industrial area with not much going on.

</Review>

<Review title="Favorite part" stars={4}>

It's cool to pass underneath the pipes and infrastructure leading to the oil tanks on either side of the road.

</Review>

# Memorial Dr. / Paul Dudley White

Back in the day, I would use this path to go between my work at Sullivan Square and my college campus at Northeastern. I'll still occasionally use it for going from work to Kendall Square or into downtown.

<Review title="Car Interactions" stars={5}>

Very few interactions with cars! The path follows the river and thus only has intersections in a few spots, like the Mass Ave and BU bridges. Bikes are generally expected to go with the pedestrian signal.

</Review>

<Review title="Lane Design" stars={4}>

Some parts are good, like near MIT, but some parts are a repurposed sidewalk, crumbling asphalt, or just extremely narrow. The part near the BU bridge was especially bad, but has been [rebuilt recently](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/safety-improvements-for-the-bu-bridge-corridor-of-memorial-drive) and looks quite a bit nicer now. I haven't ridden on the sections past there personally but I hear they're not in great shape.

</Review>

<Review title="Tricky Sections" stars={2}>

The drawbridge near the Longfellow Bridge is extremely narrow, to the point where two bikes cannot pass each other. Making matters worse, it's also shared with pedestrians! Thankfully, it's one of the less congested areas on the path.

</Review>

<Review title="Connections/Usefulness" stars={5}>

The path passes right through Kendall Square and the MIT campus. It also goes near the Community Path, but connecting between the two by the Museum of Science can be tricky. It's a nice connection to the separated lanes on Mass Ave heading into Boston.

</Review>

<Review title="Favorite part" stars={5}>

The separated bike and pedestrian lanes near the MIT campus. Runners often take the bike lane, but crowding is still pretty manageable! There are also nice bike signals at Ames St.

</Review>

# Storrow Dr. / Paul Dudley White

This is the counterpart to the Memorial Drive path, and runs on the opposite side of the river. I don't end up on this bridge super often, mostly since it's tricky to connect to, but it's always a lovely place to ride just for fun.

<Review title="Car Interactions" stars={5}>

No interactions with cars, at least in the part nearest Boston! The path runs along the river and goes underneath all of the bridges into Cambridge.

</Review>

<Review title="Lane Design" stars={5}>

The path is generally quite nice! Pedestrians typically use the paths closest to the riverbank, which makes the slightly-more-inland path much more empty. It can definitely be crowded in places, however.

</Review>

<Review title="Tricky Sections" stars={2}>

Just like the Memorial Drive path, it gets very narrow west of the BU bridge.

</Review>

<Review title="Connections/Usefulness" stars={4}>

Crossing over Storrow Drive into the city requires going over bridges, some of which are quite narrow and require walking your bike. This is the biggest drawback in my opinion -- it's a lovely lane, but there's just not very many good ways to get there!

</Review>

<Review title="Favorite part" stars={5}>

The recently-built bridge over Storrow Drive near the Longfellow bridge is wide and has gentle slopes that make it easy to bike over.

</Review>

# Southwest Corridor Park

I used to sometimes use this to get between Northeastern and Back Bay. It definitely gives off the vibe of being primarily a walking path, but it's still a great way in and out of the city. It runs on top of the Orange Line, and was originally planned to be the site of a highway leading into Boston which was thankfully cancelled.

<Review title="Car Interactions" stars={3}>

The route has relatively frequent crossings, they're a mixed bag overall. Some great with speed humps, some awkward layouts and poorly placed crosswalks.

</Review>

<Review title="Lane Design" stars={3}>

You can tell the path is mostly built for walking. The parts west of Mass Ave are better at having separated bike infrastructure, but much of it is a sidewalk.

</Review>

<Review title="Tricky Sections" stars={3}>

The area in front of Renaissance Park Garage is basically just a narrow sidewalk, and cars quickly pulling out of the garage make the situation even more sketchy.

</Review>

<Review title="Connections/Usefulness" stars={4}>

The path goes from Back Bay Station through the south side of the Northeastern University campus and out into Roxbury.

</Review>

<Review title="Favorite part" stars={4}>

I really like the new well-marked, separated lane that runs from Carter Field to the EXP building.

</Review>

# Commercial St (Boston)

This is a fun, calm, but slightly indirect way to get from the North End to Long Wharf, and is a great route to connect through if you're coming from north of the city and headed towards South Station, for instance. The route runs along the waterfront, past the park where the molasses flood happened and various piers. It also runs past a bike rental store that I've used before.

<Review title="Car Interactions" stars={4}>

It's mostly fine. The traffic signals don't separate cars making left/right turns across the path, and having a two-way path directly next to the street often means cars don't expect bikes to be going in the direction they are.

</Review>

<Review title="Lane Design" stars={4.5}>

The asphalt is great and the markings are great! It's a decent width for a two-way lane, but narrower sidewalks in the area mean that pedestrians often encroach onto the bike lane space.

</Review>

<Review title="Tricky Sections" stars={4}>

The connection to the protected bike lanes on the North Washington St bridge is really a missed opportunity -- it requires a bit of on-road riding past the ice rink.

</Review>

<Review title="Connections/Usefulness" stars={3}>

It's much more roundabout of a routing than going down Surface Rd and ultimately brings you to the same place, so it's mostly useful if you're willing to trade a bit of travel time for a nicer route. There are a pretty limited number of BlueBikes stations in the area, which is a missed opportunity.

</Review>

<Review title="Favorite part" stars={3}>

I love the extensive use of bicycle traffic signals throughout the corridor.

</Review>
